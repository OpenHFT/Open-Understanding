= Open Understanding

Understanding what is needed, why it is needed, and what should not be built
has never been more important.

Generative AI can produce code, specifications, tests, and documentation faster
than a team can honestly understand, review, and support them. Open
Understanding is a workflow for optimising the complete path from an idea to
validated production value, rather than merely increasing the rate at which
artefacts are generated.

The primary production artefact is shared understanding. Code, specifications,
tests, demonstrations, decision records, and review notes are evidence that
this understanding has been expressed accurately.

This public edition is deliberately compact. It currently publishes the core
principles, workflow, and adoption guidance. Executable demonstration projects
are not included until they are ready for independent publication review.

== Core Principles

Understanding is the challenge::
Most of the important work is discovering what should change, why it should
change, and what should remain unchanged. Faster production does not remove
this work.

Record rationale, not only behaviour::
Maintainers need to know why a choice was made, which alternatives were
considered, and what evidence would justify revisiting it.

Review transfers understanding and ownership::
Review should enable another person to explain, challenge, and support a
change. Automated checks and AI reviews can assist, but they do not accept
responsibility for the result.

Measure value separately from volume::
More generated content, commits, tests, or completed tasks do not necessarily
mean better user or business outcomes. Measure the outcome and the lifetime
ownership burden as well as delivery activity.

Make status, provenance, and ownership visible::
Exploratory, proposed, accepted, generated, superseded, and archived material
should not be indistinguishable. File layout and format may signal status, but
review history and named ownership are stronger evidence.

Prefer deterministic mechanisms for stable decisions::
Use LLMs where flexible exploration is valuable. Once a bounded decision is
well understood, consider replacing repeated model judgement with inspectable,
reproducible rules or conventional software.

State critical intent in more than one form::
Important behaviour is easier to challenge when expressed through
complementary forms such as prose, examples, invariants, tests, and diagrams.
Keep one authority for facts that must not diverge.

Passing tests is not the same as being fit for purpose::
Tests verify encoded expectations. Validation must also ask whether the right
problem was solved in the intended environment for the people affected.

Apply editorial judgement to generated writing::
AI is useful for coverage, alternatives, and a first draft. Retain only what
serves the reader and the argument; completeness is not the same as usefulness.

Preserve the important details at every elevation::
A large body of polished material can still omit the few high-level decisions
or low-level constraints that matter. Readers need the salient facts at the
level at which they are making a decision.

Let readers reconstruct the decision trail::
Record the evidence, assumptions, alternatives, decision, and consequences
needed to challenge or continue the work. This is not a demand to retain or
publish exhaustive private deliberation.

Define project intent and manage drift explicitly::
Tie work to an intended outcome. When evidence changes the intent, record that
change; when adjacent work becomes substantial, consider separating it.

Treat specifications as hypotheses::
Specifications make expectations reviewable, but they are not automatically
correct or actionable. Test, reduce, and revise them as implementation and
user evidence improve understanding.

Size changes by conceptual load::
Lines and files are poor proxies for review effort. Prefer changes that a
reviewer can understand as one coherent set of behaviour, risks, and decisions.

Validate value and define success::
Success is an observed improvement in a worthwhile outcome, considered against
the human attention, risk, and ongoing support obligation consumed.

summary and review questions.

== Workflow

Open Understanding uses six phases. The phases are a thinking aid, not a rule
that every task must follow at the same depth.

Phase 0 — Research and evidence::
Challenge the initial idea, examine existing evidence and alternatives, and
state uncertainties before implementation makes one interpretation expensive.

Phase 1 — Working requirements and first proof of concept::
Build enough in a safe environment to discover feasibility, hidden constraints,
and which candidate requirements deserve to survive.

Phase 2 — Rewrite and consolidate::
Use what was learned to produce a smaller, coherent specification and reference
implementation without preserving accidental prototype structure.

Phase 3 — Refactor towards the intended design::
Reduce the structural and conceptual distance between the reference result and
a supportable production design while protecting observed behaviour.

Phase 4 — Take ownership of release material::
Retain only behaviour the responsible people understand and are prepared to
support. Reconstruct or rewrite critical release material where that is needed
to establish genuine ownership.

Phase 5 — Independent review, release, and outcome validation::
Establish independent understanding, operational readiness, and a feedback loop
from the intended outcome to observed results.

See the link:docs/workflow/README.md[workflow overview] for phase goals, exit
questions, and proportionate use.

== Applying Open Understanding

Start with a bounded change and add controls in proportion to consequence and
reversibility. Keep human responsibility explicit, ask AI to produce evidence
rather than confidence, and stop when additional artefacts no longer improve a
decision.

The link:docs/guides/README.md[practical guidance] provides a compact adoption
checklist covering roles, evidence, documentation, risk, and the use of several
agents.

The complete public documentation map is in
link:docs/README.md[docs/README.md].

== Status and Limits

Open Understanding is advisory guidance. It does not replace legal,
contractual, security, safety, accessibility, or sector-specific obligations.
AI capabilities and failure modes change; responsibility for a maintained
system remains with the people and organisations that choose to build, release,
and operate it.
